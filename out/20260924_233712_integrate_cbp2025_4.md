### Root Cause
The regression stemmed from how the host predictor (TAGE) re-evaluates its own prediction confidence at update time. TAGE calls `predict_using_given_hist(..., false)` during the commit/update stage to recalculate `LSUM`, which determines whether the statistical corrector tables need updating (`sc_update_needed`).

Because the `tomasulo_table` models an early-pipeline structure tracking *in-flight* instructions, its contents naturally decay and are overwritten by younger instructions between the prediction stage and the update stage. My `sr_predict` implementation was incorrectly recomputing `sR` from scratch using this stale, decayed update-time pipeline state when called with `pred_time_predict = false`. 

As a result:
1. The `sR` value returned to TAGE at update time was wildly different from the one returned at prediction time.
2. TAGE's `LSUM` evaluation at update time became corrupted.
3. TAGE effectively lost its ability to accurately gauge its own confidence (`abs(LSUM) < THRES`), destroying the underlying `sc_update_needed` logic for both TAGE and my port.

I modified `sr_predict` to act as a proper snapshot retrieval. When `pred_time_predict` is `false`, it no longer touches the global `tomasulo_table` or overwrites `inflight_prediction_metadata`.
